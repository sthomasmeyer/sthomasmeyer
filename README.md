### Leonid Meteors Through Orion

![Leonid Meteors Through Orion](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/dynamicimage/assets/science/cds/apod/apod/2026/november/Leonids2022_Hongyang_2880.jpg?w=2880&h=3600&fit=clip&crop=faces%2Cfocalpoint)

Where will the next meteor appear? Even during a meteor shower, it is practically impossible to know. Therefore, a good way to enjoy a meteor shower is to find a place where you can sit comfortably and monitor a great expanse of dark sky. And it may be satisfying to share this experience with a friend. The meteor shower depicted was the 2022 Leonids and the view is from Hainan, China looking out over the South China Sea. Meteor streaks captured over a few hours were isolated and added to a foreground image recorded earlier. Leonid meteor streaks trace back to the constellation of Leo and were here seen streaking across the constellation of Orion. The bright red planet Mars appears near the top of the image. The Leonids peak in mid-November every year and so will peak again this week. Bonding over their love of astronomy, the two pictured meteor enthusiasts, shown celebrating their common birthday this month, were later married.

### Merriam-Webster

**lissome** _adjective_ • /LISS-um/

Characterized by easy flexibility and grace

> _He grew up wanting to emulate the lissome skaters he saw competing on television with all their acrobatic leaps and twirls._

> “A lissome figure in a sleek black unitard, he is a solitary presence in front of the glowing, fiery red visuals being projected on-screen.” — Mary Brennan, The Herald (Glasgow, Scotland), 30 Aug. 2026

_Lissome (less commonly spelled lissom) is a gently altered form of its synonym, lithesome. While lissome tends to be the more popular choice these days, the two words have similar pasts. They both appeared in the 18th century, and they both trace back to the much older lithe, which first appeared in English during the 14th century and comes from an Old English word meaning “gentle.” Lissome most often describes bodies and their movements as graceful and flexible, but it is also frequently used to characterize nimble singing voices, writing styles, guitar playing, and more._
