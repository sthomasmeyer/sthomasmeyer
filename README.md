### A Medium-Sized Solar Flare

![A Medium-Sized Solar Flare](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/dynamicimage/assets/science/cds/apod/apod/2026/november/solar_M_flare.jpg?w=3000&h=2759&fit=clip)

What a flare for the chromatic! Today’s image combines a series of images taken over ten minutes capturing the Sun’s chromosphere, the layer of the Sun’s atmosphere above its surface where the temperature rises. Why would the Sun become hotter farther away from its core? This is potentially due to energy from the swirling magnetic fields extending up through the Sun’s atmosphere. Those same magnetic fields can twist and snap, releasing light and energetic particles as a solar flare. The H-alpha filter used for today’s image captures light from energized hydrogen atoms as they travel off the Sun’s surface during a flare on May 14, 2025. This flare was classified as an M1 based on GOES observations, meaning it was moderately bright in X-rays. M-class flares can cause radio blackouts at Earth’s poles and minor radiation storms that can harm astronauts. They aren’t even the most powerful flares! NASA, NOAA, and other agencies monitor the Sun to predict and identify such storms so we can protect humans on Earth and in space.

_Does your favorite hobby involve a horse? Whether it does or not, the word hobby is undeniably equine: it’s a shortening of the older term hobbyhorse. And in a strange etymological twist, the word hobbyhorse is itself a product of an older word hobby that in the 1400s referred to a small or medium-sized horse, especially one that moved at a gentle pace. By the mid-1500s, hobby horse was being used to refer to a horse costume worn by a person participating in a morris dance or other performance, and then to a toy consisting of a stick with a toy horse’s head at one end that a child pretends to ride. By the next century the literal horse was unneeded, and hobbyhorse could refer to a favorite pursuit or pastime—that is, our modern hobby. From pastime, the meaning of hobbyhorse was extended to “a subject that someone returns to repeatedly, especially in conversation.” This sense is typically encountered in such phrases as “get on one’s hobbyhorse” or “ride one’s hobbyhorse.”_
